# TRST Studios Zero-Capital Growth Plan

## Core Decision

Do not borrow money or order bulk inventory before a design has proof of demand. Use the existing Printify setup for direct-to-customer fulfillment, and use partnerships to borrow distribution rather than cash.

Printify Pop-Up Store has no upfront inventory cost, but profits are paid after production and shipping. It is a demand-testing and fulfillment channel, not immediate working capital.

## The Cashless Collaboration Engine

### 1. Partner-a-Drop

Offer a limited co-created apparel release to organizations with an existing audience:

- independent musicians and DJs
- barbershops and tattoo studios
- youth teams, arts programs, and community organizations
- local events, venues, and promoters
- photographers, muralists, and neighborhood storytellers

TRST provides the creative direction, art, product pages, and fulfillment workflow. The partner provides the story, audience, and launch moment. Orders remain made to order through Printify, so no one purchases bulk inventory.

**Partner offer:** a co-branded visual story, a limited merchandise page, tracked sales, and a transparent share of net profit after production, shipping discounts, refunds, and taxes.

**Rule:** use a one-page written agreement before launch. It must state artwork ownership, approval rights, profit-share calculation, launch window, use of names/logos, and cancellation terms.

### 2. The QR Retail Test

Instead of asking a boutique to buy inventory, ask a culture-forward business to host one physical sample and a framed QR code for 30 days.

- TRST supplies one sample and the display card.
- The host receives a tracked code or link.
- Customers buy directly through the existing secure checkout.
- The host earns a pre-agreed share of verified net sales.

This creates real retail evidence without a purchase order or consignment inventory. The first candidates should be businesses that fit the work naturally, not generic stores.

### 3. Sponsor the Story, Not the Shirt

Avoid a generic logo-sponsor offer. Instead ask businesses to underwrite a specific public creative outcome:

- a community portrait mini-film
- a neighborhood photo series
- a live-print or art-night activation
- a youth-art supply or mentorship component

In exchange, the supporter receives a tasteful credit, a documented impact recap, and a co-hosted launch moment. Do not make brand visibility promises that clash with the artwork or the community partner.

### 4. TRST Forge Funds the Capsule

Use the website-building capability as a short-term studio offer. Sell a limited number of paid website-launch sprints to artists, barbershops, promoters, and small businesses that need a strong online presence.

Each client deposit funds physical samples for TRST. This is cleaner than taking speculative debt because the work creates both cash flow and a network of future Partner-a-Drop candidates.

**Offer structure:** fixed scope, 50% deposit before work begins, one week build window, clear handoff, optional monthly care plan.

## 30-Day Sequence

### Days 1-3: Choose the proof piece

Lead with Better Together Tee, Sin Miedo Tee, and No Bad Days Tee. Pick one as the campaign hero based on the strongest short-form video or community story.

### Days 4-7: Build the partner kit

Prepare one page with:

- why the collaboration fits
- visual references and a mockup
- the made-to-order model
- the launch window
- what each side provides
- one clear next step: a 20-minute conversation

### Days 8-14: Send 20 precise invitations

Split the list into five community organizations, five independent businesses, five creators, and five culture/media connectors. Personalized outreach beats a mass message.

### Days 15-21: Run one QR retail test and one partner drop

Measure scans, product-detail views, checkout starts, email signups, and orders. Keep screenshots and testimonials.

### Days 22-30: Turn results into leverage

Use the evidence to request sample quotes, apply to wholesale platforms, and approach a higher-value retail buyer. This is when a Culture Kings conversation becomes credible.

## Funding Paths That Do Not Require Traditional Investment

### Community-backed loan: Kiva U.S.

Kiva currently offers eligible U.S. small businesses up to $15,000 at 0% interest, with no fees, collateral, or minimum credit score. It does require a private fundraising period with people who know the business, so it is best used after the first partner campaign has a concrete purpose and repayment plan.

### Free business support: SBA Resource Partners

SBDCs and SCORE provide free or low-cost counseling. Use a counselor to review unit economics, entity/tax basics, partner agreements, and the wholesale cost sheet before taking on debt.

### Arts grants: long-horizon only

Creative Capital is a strong fit for an original community-art project, but its current open call is closed. Treat grant work as a future artist-project path, not this season's operating cash.

## Do Not Do These Things

1. Do not offer a retailer wholesale pricing until a producer gives written costs and lead times.
2. Do not call a product “limited” unless the quantity or order window is truly limited.
3. Do not promise a sponsor reach, conversions, or brand visibility that the campaign cannot control.
4. Do not borrow for inventory without a cost sheet, realistic sales plan, and a repayment path.
5. Do not give away unlimited custom work in exchange for vague “exposure.”

## First Action

Build a one-page Partner-a-Drop offer for outreach, then identify the first 20 candidates by audience fit. Start with one collaboration that can launch in two weeks using Printify, not a major retailer pitch.
