# TRST Open Thread

## A New Kind of Streetwear System

**TRST Open Thread** is a city-by-city public storytelling system. It makes apparel the lasting artifact of a living local record instead of the first thing people are asked to buy.

The idea is simple:

> **A city tells TRST what it needs to remember. TRST turns that signal into a visible, limited cultural artifact.**

This is not a sponsor campaign with a logo wall. It is not a traditional pop-up. It is a repeatable protocol where a place, its people, local businesses, artists, and organizations each create a piece of a shared public thread.

## Why It Is Ownable

Most brands ask a community to buy into a finished idea. Open Thread lets the community visibly shape the evidence behind the idea before the release happens.

That changes every participant's role:

- **People** become contributors, not an audience.
- **Local spaces** become anchors, not rented venues.
- **Artists and organizations** become story stewards, not decoration.
- **Supporters** fund a visible public moment, not a vague brand promise.
- **TRST** becomes the maker and keeper of the cultural record.

The system creates a strong reason to participate because every partner receives something concrete: a place in the city record, useful content, new local connection, and a transparent recap of what their involvement helped create.

## The Core Mechanic: Signals Become Threads

Every chapter uses one local prompt. In Santa Ana:

> **"Santa Ana gave me..."**

People answer in a line, a voice note, a short portrait, or a drawing. Participation is opt-in and credited only with permission.

TRST curates selected contributions into a **City Thread**: a living digital composition, physical story wall, short video sequence, and eventually a limited apparel release.

The tee is not a printout of somebody's private story. It is the artistic response to the collective signal.

## The Four Roles

| Role | What they do | What they receive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Signal Makers** | Add a line, portrait, audio note, or creative response. | A place in the opt-in city archive, early access to the chapter reveal, and recognition if they choose it. |
| **Thread Anchors** | Offer a small physical point of connection: shop, cafe, studio, market, gallery, or event. | A co-hosted moment, a custom QR portal, recap assets, and a visible place in the chapter map. |
| **Story Stewards** | Artists, cultural groups, youth organizations, and local historians who protect context and guide the chapter. | Accurate credit, compensated collaboration when work is commissioned, reusable media, and meaningful local visibility. |
| **Current Partners** | Supply a specific resource: cash, printing, food, space, production, promotion, or access. | A fixed, approved recognition package and a transparent outcome note. |

## The Chapter Protocol

Each city chapter has five acts. It should feel like a cultural ritual, not an event calendar entry.

### 1. Listen: Seven Seed Signals

Before public launch, TRST gathers seven short signals from people already connected to the city. This avoids arriving with an empty campaign and asking strangers to do all the meaning-making.

Output: seven permissioned lines, portraits, or audio clips that create the chapter tone.

### 2. Surface: The Open Thread Table

At one existing local gathering, a minimal Story Table asks one prompt and displays the seven seed signals. Visitors can contribute in less than two minutes.

Output: new signals, voluntary email signups, portraits, and direct introductions.

### 3. Weave: The 72-Hour Public Composition

Within 72 hours, TRST publishes a first digital City Thread from the contributions. It is intentionally unfinished: the community can see its own participation becoming a visible object.

Output: short video, story carousel, web page, and partner recap preview.

### 4. Return: The Thread Reveal

TRST reveals the artistic response: a limited chapter print, small poster, or apparel design detail alongside the complete City Thread. Contributors and anchors receive it first.

Output: a release with proof of place, not generic scarcity messaging.

### 5. Leave Something Behind: The Receipt

Every chapter ends with a public **Thread Receipt**:

- how many people opted in
- which anchors hosted the work
- what support was contributed
- what creative work was created
- what was returned to the collaborators or community, if any return was promised

The Receipt is the trust engine. It turns participation into a record of follow-through.

## The Santa Ana First Chapter

### Chapter name

**Santa Ana Gave Me**

### Three micro-locations, one city thread

Instead of one big launch, use three small anchors over two weeks:

1. **GCS Clothing**: streetwear and artist seed night.
2. **DTSA ArtWalk or Cito Mercadito**: public collection of signals.
3. **MASA or Urban Arts Outreach**: context session where a story steward helps shape the return.

Each location carries the same prompt and a unique QR portal. The QR codes show which anchor created each connection without treating people like marketing inventory.

### The public artifact

A single Santa Ana Thread page displays:

- the active prompt
- a small, consented mosaic of anonymous or credited signals
- the anchor map
- the date of the next physical moment
- the chapter's Thread Receipt once complete

## What Partners Cannot Easily Copy

They can copy a pop-up. They cannot quickly copy a credible local record if TRST keeps these rules:

1. **One prompt, one place, one chapter.** No generic slogans ported everywhere.
2. **Small before loud.** Seven seed signals before public promotion.
3. **Visible receipts.** Every promise has a simple public follow-through.
4. **Partner roles are functional.** A partner provides a real capability, not just a logo.
5. **The archive compounds.** Santa Ana becomes proof for Los Angeles, then Oakland, without pretending all cities are the same.

## Low-Cost Operating Model

| Need | Lean version |
| --- | --- |
| Capture | Phone camera, paper cards, one consent sentence, and a QR form. |
| Display | One story wall, two clipboards, one sample tee per design, and a small table sign. |
| Digital thread | Short-form video and a dedicated web page built from approved submissions. |
| Partner proof | Five edited photos, one short clip, and one Thread Receipt. |
| Growth | Every anchor receives its own QR code and becomes the introduction path to the next anchor. |

No inventory needs to be purchased for the first chapter. The first goal is proof of participation, a strong cultural artifact, and repeatable partner trust.

## The Partner Invitation

Do not ask: "Will you sponsor us?"

Ask:

> **Would you help anchor the first Santa Ana Open Thread? We are collecting one-line stories about what the city has given its people, then returning that signal as a public creative record. We need one small place where people can add their line.**

Then describe the exact contribution needed: a table at an existing event, one introduction, a small print run, refreshments, or a $250 support slot. Make the contribution finite and the return visible.

## What To Build On The Site, Only After One Host Says Yes

1. `/open-thread/santa-ana` - public chapter page with the prompt, anchor, date, and approved contributions.
2. One partner-specific QR landing URL per anchor, using UTM tracking.
3. A two-question opt-in form: contribution and email. Add a clear optional credit field and image/voice consent separate from email consent.
4. A private contributor and partner log. Never publish personal submissions by default.
5. A public Thread Receipt page after the chapter closes.

## The Success Standard

The first chapter works if it makes one partner invite TRST back, one story steward want to shape the next thread, and one new anchor contact TRST without being cold-pitched. Sales are measured, but they are not the only proof that the system has become culturally useful.
