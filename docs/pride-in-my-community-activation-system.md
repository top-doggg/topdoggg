# Pride In My Community: Activation System

## The Big Idea

**Pride In My Community: Three Stories, One Drop** turns three TRST tees into three reasons to gather:

- **Better Together**: people and places that make community feel shared.
- **Sin Miedo**: courage, self-expression, and making work without permission.
- **No Bad Days**: joy, recovery, and choosing to keep moving.

The product is present, but it is not the opening line. The opening line is a short, local story that a partner can help make visible.

## The Smallest Viable Activation

Run one 90-minute **Story Table** at a partner space, market, studio, cafe, gallery, or community event.

| Element | What happens | Cost-conscious version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story wall | Guests write one sentence beginning with "My community taught me..." | Kraft paper, markers, clips, and a QR sign. |
| Portrait corner | Take a simple portrait or short vertical video of volunteers who opt in. | Phone, natural light, plain fabric backdrop. |
| Tee table | Display the three designs with one sentence of context each. | Samples, QR codes to product pages, no inventory commitment. |
| Partner moment | Partner names a local person, organization, or story worth recognizing. | 5-minute conversation or recorded quote. |
| Call to action | QR code for email list and a post-event drop page. | One unique QR code per partner, tracked with UTM links. |

Success is not measured by a crowded photo. Success is a partner relationship, 15-30 email signups, 8-12 permissioned portraits or stories, product-page visits, and one follow-up opportunity.

## Partner Lanes

### 1. Host Partners

Spaces that can offer a corner, table, calendar listing, or market placement.

- Ask: "Can we host a 90-minute Story Table during an existing event?"
- Give: quality community content, an easy participant experience, post-event recap, and co-branded recognition.
- Best examples: The LAB Anti-Mall, OPEN LA, Days, Firehouse Projects.

### 2. Story Partners

Artists, youth programs, community groups, neighborhood organizers, and cultural organizations.

- Ask: "Who or what story should we honor in this drop?"
- Give: an accurate spotlight, participant credits, content they can reuse, and a community donation or in-kind contribution only when it is defined and sustainable.
- Best examples: Youth Beat, Transformation Arts, Costa Mesa Community Foundation, Arts Orange County.

### 3. Support Partners

Local businesses, larger brands, service providers, and cultural institutions that can supply money, food, print, space, audience, or promotion.

- Ask: one defined contribution: $250-$500 activation support, a venue corner, print materials, refreshments, photographer time, or promotion.
- Give: a fixed return: mention in one recap, logo on event signage, 3-5 usable event photos, one short content clip, and a post-event scorecard.
- Never promise reach, sales, or exclusivity that TRST cannot verify.

## The Partner Offer: Three Simple Levels

| Level | Contribution | What the partner receives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Host** | Space, table, event listing, or staff introduction | Co-branded Story Table, 5 edited photos, recap credit, tracked QR results. |
| **Story Supporter** | $250-$500 or equivalent in-kind support | Everything in Host, plus logo on event card and one 15-30 second recap clip. |
| **Presenting Partner** | $1,000 or clearly equivalent support | Everything in Story Supporter, campaign-level recognition, and a private scorecard after all three stories. |

These are conversation anchors, not invoices. Use them only after the partner asks how they can help.

## Outreach Sequence

### Step 1: Personal Connection

Research one real event, program, artist, or community value before writing. Contact one person through a public business email, contact form, LinkedIn, or Instagram.

**Subject:** A small community-story activation at [Organization]

> Hi [Name],
>
> I’m [Your Name], building TRST Studios, an artist-led apparel project rooted in place, cultural memory, and visual storytelling. I saw [specific program, event, or value], and it feels close to the kind of work we want to support.
>
> We’re starting a small series called *Pride In My Community: Three Stories, One Drop*. The idea is a simple 90-minute Story Table where guests add a line about their community, take an optional portrait, and meet the story behind three original tee designs.
>
> I think [Organization] could be a natural home for one session. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation next week to see if a lightweight collaboration fits your calendar?
>
> [Name]
> TRST Studios
> https://trststudios.online/partners

### Step 2: Follow-up After Five Business Days

> Hi [Name], just bringing this back up in case it got buried. We can make this very small: one table, 90 minutes, no inventory obligation, and we handle the setup and recap. If it is not a fit, I would still appreciate knowing who on your team handles local programming or partnerships.

### Step 3: Share a One-Page Concept

Only send the sponsor brief after interest is returned. Include the exact date window, location need, who TRST will bring, who owns what, and the single contribution being requested.

### Step 4: Confirm in Writing

Use a plain confirmation email, even for a friendly collaboration:

- date, time, location, and load-in
- partner contribution and TRST contribution
- approved use of logos, images, and names
- whether participant portraits are opt-in and how releases are handled
- the single success metric both sides care about
- cancellation contact and backup plan

## Relationship Rhythm

| Moment | TRST action |
| --- | --- |
| Before the activation | Share a one-page run-of-show and simple assets for approval. |
| During | Credit partner in every public post; collect only opt-in email and portrait permissions. |
| Within 48 hours | Thank-you note, 3-5 preview images, and a short recap. |
| Within 10 days | One-page scorecard: attendance estimate, opt-ins, content assets, QR visits, checkout starts, and top audience response. |
| Within 30 days | Ask for the next collaboration, referral, or a second location. |

| Week | Outcome |

### Santa Ana Story Prompt

Use this local prompt at the first table:

> **"Santa Ana gave me..."**

Guests finish the sentence on the story wall. It is specific enough to feel local and open enough for many lived experiences. The three best permissioned responses become the campaign's first short posts.

### Santa Ana Partner Map

- **Start with GCS:** immediate design and streetwear credibility.
- **Use DTSA ArtWalk or Cito Mercadito:** build natural foot traffic without buying an audience.
- **Bring in MASA or Urban Arts Outreach:** ensure the storytelling piece is real community participation, not an aesthetic overlay.
- **Use 4th Street Market:** scalable downtown venue route after the first proof point.
- **Approach Fashion Week last:** it is a visibility accelerator, not the place to discover the concept.

## Guardrails

- Do not call a partner a sponsor until they agree in writing.
- Do not sell a participant's story, image, or personal information without clear written permission.
- Do not use cause language, donations, or beneficiary claims unless the amount, recipient, and timing are defined.
- Keep the first activation small enough to execute beautifully with current capacity.
- Make every collaboration useful to the partner even if it produces no immediate sales.
